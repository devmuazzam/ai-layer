# The Incident Root Cause Report (IRCR)

**What this is.** TCG's own incident investigation template, branded *The Crane Guys*, version
"Updated 2026". It is the **"IRCR" PowerPoint deck** that Safety described at the 29 Sep 2026 safety
meeting: *"the investigation is written up as a PowerPoint deck with images"*
([../../meetings/2026-09-29-safety-discussion-onboarding-certifications-incidents-and-facility-compliance.md](../../meetings/2026-09-29-safety-discussion-onboarding-certifications-incidents-and-facility-compliance.md) §5.2).

**Dee's rule, verbatim (2026-11-16):** *"This is used as a guide for our incident and injury
investigations deck. Every step must be completed per the instructions in the deck to close the
investigation."*

**How it relates to the older form.** [04-incident-investigation-form.md](04-incident-investigation-form.md)
is a generic contractor's form of unknown use. The IRCR is TCG's current, branded template, and Dee
names it as what closes an investigation. **Reading, not confirmed:** the IRCR is the standard and
form 04 is not in use.

**A template, not a spec.** It says what TCG records and in what order. It does not say what the
ERP must build. Today the build has only a small incident report on a job ticket: type, severity,
description, signature and attachments (`TicketIncidentReport`).

## The six steps

| Step | Slides | What it records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1–3 | Cover: **report no., date of incident, prepared by**. Ground rules (below). |
| 1 Respond & report | 4 | **Immediate actions**, each with a done box and a **time**: scene made safe and care given (911 if needed); supervisor and Safety & Compliance Manager notified; scene left undisturbed and photographed before anything moves; witnesses identified and kept available; post-incident testing per company policy or DOT. **Agency and party notifications**, each with *required?*, date/time and contact/reference #: **Cal/OSHA** (death or serious injury: immediately, within 8 hours); **Federal OSHA** for non-California sites (death 8 h; hospitalization, amputation, eye loss 24 h); **DOT/FMCSA, CHP or local police** (public-road crash; DOT-recordable crashes go on the register); **client, site owner and insurance carrier** (per contract and policy). |
| 2 Document the facts | 5 | Date, time; employee(s) involved (name and job title); supervisor; location (site, facility or road); unit/area/equipment; task being performed; **incident type**: Injury, Vehicle crash, Property damage, Near miss, Other; **outcome**: No injury, First aid, Medical treatment, Hospitalization, Fatality; type of injury (body part and nature); *new, different or unusual?* (crew, equipment, weather, schedule, procedure); incident summary (facts only, in order). |
| 3 Timeline & evidence | 6–7 | **Timeline**: time, what happened (fact), source (person, record, photo #). **Witnesses**: name and role, phone/e-mail, where they were, statement signed (date). **Photo log**: photo, time, by, shows (wide, medium, close-up; originals unedited; more slides as needed). **Records to collect**: training records and certifications; equipment inspection and maintenance logs; job hazard analysis / pre-task plan; lift plan, rigging plan or load chart; driver logs, ELD, GPS or dash-cam; permits, work orders and procedures in effect. |
| 4 Root cause | 8–9 | **Five Whys**: problem, then Why 1–5, each with *because* (a fact) and *evidence* (photo #, statement or record), then **root cause**. One chain per cause path (duplicate the slide). Stop at a fixable system, procedure, equipment or training cause, *"not at a person's mistake"*. **Four validation tests**, yes/no: reverse test; every answer evidenced; would fixing it have prevented the incident; system focus. If any is "No", revise. **Context**: other contributing factors; standards and procedures that apply. |
| 5 Fix it for good | 10–11 | **Hierarchy of controls**, strongest first: Eliminate, Substitute, Engineering, Administrative, PPE. **Action plan**: action, which Why it fixes, control level, **owner**, **due**. **Interim protection** until the fix is done. **Sustainability check**, five yes/no questions (fixes the root cause; doesn't depend on memory; built into procedures, training, inspections or planning; every action has an owner and due date; crews and clients told). **Prove it's working** check-ins at **30 days, 90 days and 6 months**: date, what was checked, effective yes/no, verified by. |
| 6 Review & sign-off | 12 | Four signatures with name and date: **prepared by (investigator)**, **reviewed by supervisor**, **reviewed by Safety & Compliance Manager**, **approved by management**. A six-item pre-sign checklist. *"Sign only after every step and attachment is complete. Never change a signed report — add a dated addendum."* Filing: the signed report and all attachments saved under the report number, kept as long as policy and regulation require. |
| — | 13 | Presenter's guide: present facts, timeline, evidence, root cause, fix; what to bring; speaking tips. |

## Ground rules printed in the template (slide 3)

- **Do:** write only what was seen, measured or documented; date, time and initial every entry;
  quote witnesses in their own words; reference the evidence behind each claim; **fix errors with a
  dated addendum, never by overwriting**; start right away.
- **Don't:** speculate; assign blame or use legal terms; move, edit or delete original photos, notes
  or records; lead witnesses; add medical detail beyond injury type and treatment level; share drafts
  outside the investigation team.
- *"If a claim, citation, or lawsuit is possible: contact legal counsel before you finalize or share
  this report."*
