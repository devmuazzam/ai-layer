# Customer-imposed safety requirements

**What this is.** Three executed documents in which a **customer imposes safety obligations on TCG as
a contractor**:

| Document | Counterparty | Dated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `COSP_CraneGuys_CraneServices_CW2285198` | **Southern California Edison** | plan v1.0 (Sep 2023), this instance updated **2025-12-09** |
| `SubSafety Agreement — The Crane Guys — H&M Santa Barbara — Job 1140278C` | **Mesa Energy Systems** (as prime) | signed **2026-03-06 / 2026-04-06** |
| `Mesa Energy Attachment 1 — Subcontract Schedule` | **Mesa Energy Systems** | **2026-04-03** |

**Why it matters.** Everything else in `safety-compliance/` is TCG's own programme. **This is the
programme other people require of TCG**, in writing, with named signatories and a purchase reference
— and it is a **different obligation per customer**. It is also the reason ticket 44944 exists: that
ticket is a Southern California Edison pole swap, worked under the SCE plan below.

> **Executed contract documents, not requirements.** These describe obligations TCG has already
> accepted with particular customers. Nothing in them has been adjudicated as ERP scope. Personal
> contact details (phone numbers, email addresses) printed in the SCE plan are **deliberately not
> copied**; names and titles are recorded because the roles matter.

---

## 1. Southern California Edison — Contractor Orientation and Safety Plan

A **71-page** SCE-owned form, filled in by SCE and by TCG, and signed by both before work starts.

> *"This document must identify relevant safety programs, procedures, mitigation measures, and
> approaches put in place to address potential hazards in the work performed... The Plan shall be
> updated as needed (e.g., when any component changes or when additional hazard mitigations are
> required) but at a minimum it shall be **reviewed and updated (and dated/signed) annually**."*

### The engagement, as recorded

| Project Name | **SCE Crane Operations** |
| Purchasing Reference | `CW2285198; C4083` |
| **Source Work?** | **Y** — *"Repetitive project work under an agreement that lasts for an extended period"* |
| Anticipated start → completion | **2024-10-01 → 2027-09-30** |
| Project Location | SCE Service Territory |
| Contractor | The Crane Guys, LLC |
| **Safety Tier** | **☒ Safety Tier 1 Higher Risk (HR)** (of Tier 1 HR / Tier 1 / Tier 2) |

**The Safety Tier drives the obligation.** Tier 1 and Tier 1 HR work requires the hazard assessment
in Section 13 to be completed by both parties, and *"Safety Tier 1 Contractors shall upload this
signed document to the **TPA**"* (third-party administrator). Tier 2 does not.

### Scope of work, as SCE describes it

Crane and rigging services and self-loader trucks across the **entire SCE service territory**;
cranes, labour, rigging and traffic control as directed by SCE Crane Operations; *"various terrain
including residential, hillsides, rocky terrain and **night work**"*; **24/7 and emergency basis**;
*"scheduled and emergency call-out services on 24-hour, 7 days per week, 365 days per year basis"*;
transport of SCE-owned material by self-loader; and equipment including self-loaders, tractors, stake
beds, trailers (flatbed, lowboy, double-drop, step deck, specialty), pallet jacks, pilot cars, tools,
straps, chains, dunnage and snooter boards. *"Contractor shall provide **only crane operators, riggers
and drivers which are qualified and experienced** to operate contractors' equipment."*

### The pre-pick tailboard script

TCG's answer to SCE's tailboard-protocol section is a **twelve-point script**, reproduced because
it is the only place in the vault a tailboard is written as words to be said:

> 1. Everyone attends
> 2. **The Operator is 100% in charge of the lift process** and will be overseeing the lift today
> 3. Only the **designated signal man** can give directions to the operator — *"Radio communication
>    will / will not be used"*
> 4. All communication to the operator goes through the signal man
> 5. **All rigging must be approved by the operator**
> 6. The lift plan for today is discussed... and everyone will know what they are doing
> 7. Never walk under or work under the load
> 8. **STOP Work Authority, anyone can stop the work**
> 9. Never wrap the tag line around your wrist, arm and hand
> 10. Stay focused on pinch points and load when in flight
> 11. *"Does everyone understand and agree with what I just said?"*
> 12. **"Please sign the Pre-Lift meeting form."**

Point 12 is the JSA signature. Point 2 matches the JSA form's *"Responsible person on site will be
the crane operator"*.

### PPE, communications, and hazards

**PPE** is answered item by item, each with a *"Contractor Safety Program Reference"* and a
*"Contractor Requirement"*. Ticked: Head, Face, Eye, Hand, Hearing, Fall, Foot Protection,
**AR/FR Clothing**, High Visibility Clothing, and **Barricades and Signs** (*"Barricade chains or
cones should be placed around crane when in operation; signage for traffic and pedestrian control
should be used if not provided by the customer's designated traffic control vendor"*). Unticked:
Respiratory Protection, Personal Flotation Devices. **Every reference points at the same source: "The
Crane Guys (TCG's) Safety Manual and IIPP" — which is not in this drop.**

**Jobsite communications** are answered as Verbal, **Radio & Satellite Phones** (*"In areas where
cellular service is unreliable or unavailable"*), **GPS Tracking** (*"to monitor the location and
movement of workers, particularly in remote or hazardous environments"*) and Electronic. Note the
premise: SCE work is expected to happen **where there is no cell service** — which is the offline
requirement, from the customer's side.

**Section 13, Hazard Assessment and Mitigation**, is the bulk of the document: SCE selects each
applicable *Primary Hazard / Activity / Condition*, supplies pre-agreed **Critical Observable Actions
(COAs)**, and TCG writes a mitigation plan against each with a reference to its own safety programme.
The hazards ticked for this scope:

```
Basic Site Safety · General Safety · Hand and Power Tools · Fire · Flammable/Combustible Liquids
Traffic · Pedestrians · Tripping/Impalement · Human Performance · Ergonomic Risk · Sanitation
Communication Limitations · Contaminated Soil · Weather Conditions · Environmental Conditions
Remote Work · Emergency Evacuation Limitations · Noise
```

with mitigations including *"Every crane and trailer has a spill kit on board"*, use of **Roadway
Construction Services** for traffic control, and hand signals where visibility permits.

**Worker orientation and oversight** (Section 10) records three TCG commitments in TCG's own words:
*"TCG holds a **minimum of quarterly safety meetings**... All field and applicable employees attend
this safety meeting/training where they learn the content of the SCE COSP. Have Specific training
cover all new hires and once yearly."* · a new-hire orientation with an assigned supervisor, regular
check-ins and a **6-month performance review** · and — this one is operational — *"Contractor shall
ensure that all copies of the current signed copy and other reference documentation are **stored
securely in the cab of the equipment at the work location of each crew**."*

**Active subcontractors** (Section 11) is a live register the prime must keep current, with columns
`Company · Scope · Safety Tier · ISN# · ISN Grade · Representative · Orientation Date`. Two are
listed: **Roadway Construction Service** (permitting and traffic control, Tier 1, ISN grade **A**,
oriented 2024-10-07) and **Piazza Trucking** (trucking services, Tier 1, ISN grade **C**, oriented
2024-10-08).

> **`ISN` is a third-party contractor-prequalification service and the grade is a letter.** TCG holds
> a safety grade for each of its own subcontractors, per customer. Piazza Trucking already appears in
> the vault on the accounting side
> ([vendors-agencies/outside-trucking-piazza-invoice-sop.md](../../vendors-agencies/outside-trucking-piazza-invoice-sop.md));
> this is the same vendor with a safety tier, an ISN number and an orientation date attached.
> Nothing in `vendors-agencies/` models vendor safety qualification.

**Emergency action plan** — the plan requires named clinics with addresses and *"maps/directions"*,
posted where all workers can access them, per yard/facility. Two clinics are named here. The demo
build carries a `hospital` block on its ticket; the field JSA form asks for
`Nearest Hospital Address` and `Distance`.

## 2. Mesa Energy / H&M Santa Barbara — the subcontract pair

Two documents for one job: **H&M Santa Barbara, 829 State St**, Mesa's job `#1140278C`, TCG as
subcontractor to Mesa Energy Systems under a **Master Subcontract Agreement of 2015-10-30**.

### The Subcontract Schedule (2026-04-03)

A short instrument that binds one location under the master agreement. What matters for the ERP:

- **Compensation `$22,100.42`**, `Terms Net 30 or 2% Net 10` — but the payment clause says Mesa pays
  *"within **forty-five (45) days** following receipt of such invoice(s)"*. **The stated terms and
  the stated payment window disagree on the face of the document.**
- **Additional insureds** — TCG must furnish certificates naming Mesa for CGL and Automobile
  Liability.
- **`Certified Payroll Required: Yes ___ No _X_`** — a printed, per-schedule switch. See
  [payroll/certified-payroll-playbook.md](../../payroll/certified-payroll-playbook.md); this is the
  contract-level field that turns it on.
- **Flow-down** — *"all provisions therein that are required to be incorporated herein, are hereby
  deemed to be so incorporated... In the event of any conflict... the provision imposing the **more
  stringent** measures and/or **higher standard of care** shall apply."*
- Signed for TCG by **Melissa McNutt, President**; Contract `PO #145454`.

### The Sub-Contractor Safety Agreement (signed 2026-03-06 / 2026-04-06)

Nine numbered items, **each individually initialled** by TCG's representative. In summary:

1. **Site-specific orientation before any work begins**, in addition to any client/owner orientation;
   all emergency protocols (evacuation locations, contact numbers, alarms, procedures) addressed, and
   *"any and all revisions shall be documented in weekly safety toolbox meeting"*.
2. Observe **three** safety programmes at once — the **Client/Owner's**, **Mesa's**, and all federal,
   state and local law including federal and state OSHA — each acknowledged with its own initial, and
   Cal/OSHA acknowledged against a named published guide.
3. **Immediately notify Mesa and supply copies of any incident, illness, near-miss, and employee
   safety-compliance disciplinary action record**, and supply personnel to participate in the
   investigation.
4. Supply Mesa with copies of **site-specific training records, weekly site safety inspections,
   weekly site safety meetings, and Pre-Task Safety Plans or Job Hazard Analysis**.
5. Supply and train on required PPE. Minimum: **safety glasses (100% on-site requirement), hard hat,
   work boots, clothing suitable for construction work**.
6. All tools and equipment present, in good working order, **regularly inspected per
   OSHA/manufacturer requirements, and properly labelled and stored**.
7. Good housekeeping at all times.
8. **Stop-work on non-compliance** — *"Any failure to correct unsafe acts and/or unsafe conditions
   will result in the **stoppage of the subcontractor's work** until... corrected to the satisfaction
   of Mesa Energy Systems Management."*
9. **Dismissal and removal from site** for workers or supervisors who repeatedly fail to comply.

> **Items 3 and 4 are outbound reporting obligations on a per-job basis.** TCG must send a customer
> its incident reports, its near-misses, its **employee disciplinary records**, its training records,
> its weekly inspections and meetings, and its JSAs. Nothing in the vault models a document that has
> to be produced *for a customer*, on a schedule, from safety data. The value engine's `Monthly
> Customer Reporting` box and the Quarterly Safety Value Report are TCG's own version of this;
> **item 4 is a customer's version, with a different content list and a weekly cadence.**

## 4. Open points

Recorded for a human to route; **not** written to any `gaps.md`.

1. **Does a customer record carry safety obligations?** SCE requires a signed plan in the cab, an
   annual review and a tier; Mesa requires per-job document delivery. Neither `customers/` nor
   field-ops models any of it.
2. **`Certified Payroll Required: Yes/No` is a per-schedule contract switch.** Does that field exist
   on the job or the customer in the ERP, and does it drive the payroll module?
3. Should the app be able to **surface the customer's safety documents to the crew** — the "signed
   copy in the cab" obligation is a document-availability requirement, and FCC already has an
   `Include On Unit` file-links mechanism ([../fcc-nexgen-service-guide.md](../fcc-nexgen-service-guide.md)).
4. **Vendor safety qualification** — ISN number, ISN grade, safety tier, orientation date — has no
   home in `vendors-agencies/`.
5. Does the ERP need to **produce customer safety packets** (incidents, training records, weekly
   inspections, JSAs) on a per-customer schedule?
6. **TCG's Safety Manual and IIPP is cited as the authority behind every mitigation and is not in the
   vault.** Request it.
7. The Mesa schedule's `Net 30 or 2% Net 10` versus its 45-day payment clause is an internal
   contradiction in an executed document — worth confirming which governs.
