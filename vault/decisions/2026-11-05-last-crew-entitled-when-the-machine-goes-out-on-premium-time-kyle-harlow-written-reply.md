# Last-crew entitlement: the test is whether the machine goes out on premium time

*`verified` here means that Kyle Harlow wrote this reply. It does not mean the rule has been checked
against the union agreement, and it does not settle the open items below. `supersedes` above is
**in part**: only the trigger in item 3 of the 2026-11-03 note (see* What this changes*).*

## Decision

In Kyle Harlow's words, what the reply settles:

1. **The test is the machine's next departure.** *"If that machine goes out on premium time. During
   the week this is between 5 PM and 6 AM. On the weekend it is considered all of Sat all of Sunday."*
2. **Hours worked do not matter.** *"Whether they work four hours eight hours or 15 hours."*
3. **The right sits with the crew on the last straight shift.** *"The crew that was on the crane on
   the last straight shift is entitled to the machine first."* A second wording of who holds it is in
   the same reply (open item 1).
4. **Not entitled on a straight-shift departure.** *"The only way the crew that was on the machine
   last straight shift is not entitled to the machine as if it's leaving during a straight shift
   between 6 AM and 5 PM."* Open item 2 is the tension with his sentence on assigned cranes.
5. **The machine sitting does not end it.** *"Even if the machine sat for three days during normal
   shift and goes out on the weekend or another premium time shift, the crew still is entitled."*
6. **Operator and oiler only.** *"only operator and oiler are entitled to this. no third men or
   trucking have this entitlement."*
7. **An automatic reminder to dispatch.** *"Yes, I believe it should be an automatic reminder to
   Dispatch, who was on the machine last"*. The *"Yes"* answers a question we do not have.
8. **Both are contacted, by text and by phone call, and whoever responds is entitled.** *"Both the
   operator and the oiler would receive a text message and phone call if one or the other response or
   both whoever responds is entitled to the work."*
9. **A crew not assigned to the crane is offered it first on a premium departure.** *"they have to be
   given the opportunity first."* His next sentence, *"If they decline it or they do not respond in a
   timely manner"*, breaks off without saying what follows. The only stated consequence is on emergency
   work (item 10).
10. **15 minutes is the only number, and only for emergency work within four hours.** *"the only hard
    number we have here for is for emergency jobs which after they've been contacted through text and
    phone call they have 15 minutes to respond. That is only for work that is going to be in the next
    four hours or less."* After that, *"the Dispatch can look for someone else to do the work."*

## The reply — verbatim

## What this changes

- **Replaces the trigger in item 3 of the 2026-11-03 note.** That item, Kyle's one-line Yes to a
  restatement, read: the crew finished on overtime and the crane goes out before 6 AM; at 6 or later,
  anyone. This reply tests the machine's next departure, not how the crew's last shift ended. A crew
  that finished on straight time and a crane leaving at 7 PM on a weekday give different results under
  the two. Kyle does not name the 2026-11-03 message or say *"I withdraw"*; he opens *"a little bit of
  misunderstanding"* and does not say whose. The 2026-10-22 wording that the restatement came from is
  his own (*"It's only if they ended on an overtime"*,
  [../meetings/transcripts/2026-10-22-erp-demo-walkthrough-part-2.md](../meetings/transcripts/2026-10-22-erp-demo-walkthrough-part-2.md),
  1:09:32). Marking this as a replacement is recorded at Syed Haris's request and on the text above.
- **Makes the crew-or-machine question moot as asked.** The 2026-11-03 note left open whether *overtime*
  was the crew's or the machine's. The test now reads *"that machine goes out on premium time"*, and a
  crew's overtime is no longer part of it.
- **Narrows *"at 6 or later"*.** That limb had no AM or PM. It is now *"between 6 AM and 5 PM"*.
- **Item 1 of the 2026-11-03 note stands, and is firmer.** The reminder is repeated as *"automatic"*.
  Which crew it shows is open item 1.
- **Item 2 of the 2026-11-03 note is untouched and unconfirmed.** He neither repeats nor withdraws the
  yes/no record (open item 9).
- **Adds to how the crew is reached.** The 2026-11-03 plan was a reminder to *call*; he now says text
  message and phone call. The 2026-04-09 note, which said only that offers are recorded in the system,
  stands.

## What was not decided

Nothing below is filled in here. Each is written so that a short reply settles it.

1. **Which crew, when the wordings point at different people.** *"the crew that was on the crane on the
   last straight shift"*, against *"whoever was on the machine last"* and *"who was on the machine
   last"*. If Crew A ran the crane's last straight shift, Crew B then ran it on premium, and it goes out
   on premium again, who gets called first: Crew A or Crew B?
2. **Assigned versus not assigned.** An assigned crew *"automatically go out whether it's on premium
   time or on a straight shift"*, while *"the only way"* the last straight-shift crew loses it is a
   straight-shift departure. How do those fit? What makes a crane a crew's *"assigned crane"*? Is
   *"automatically"* still an offer they may refuse (he follows it with first right of refusal)? The
   vault has no definition: see [../dispatch/gaps.md](../dispatch/gaps.md) → *Default crew per unit,
   overridable per job*, itself open.
3. **The boundaries.** Is a departure at exactly 5:00 PM or exactly 6:00 AM premium or straight? Which
   moment is tested: leaving the yard, the job start, or the scheduled time (he says *"goes out"* and
   *"leaving"*)? Does a shift that starts on straight time and runs into premium count as going out on
   premium?
4. **Holidays.** He names *"all of Sat all of Sunday"* and no holidays. The union clause names them. Do
   holidays count as premium for this rule?
5. **"Timely manner" on non-emergency work.** No number, and his sentence does not say what dispatch may
   do once a crew declines or does not respond on non-emergency work. **What counts as an emergency** is
   also not defined: is all work within four hours an emergency, or only some of it? Nearby and not adopted: on
   2026-04-09 Kyle said the 15 minutes is *"only for emergency work"*, for work *"after 6:00 PM that came
   in and it wasn't planned. If it's planned stuff, they have longer"*
   ([transcript](../meetings/transcripts/2026-04-09-dispatch-scheduling-figma-demo.md), 1:24:42–1:24:55).
6. **Who sends the text and makes the call: dispatch or the system?** *"would receive"* does not say.
   Nearby and not decided: [../erp-review-log.md](../erp-review-log.md) #1 (`unverified`) and the
   2026-04-09 note, last item.
7. **If only one of operator and oiler responds, who fills the other seat?** Anyone dispatch picks, or
   someone else first?
8. **The pay owed if the crew is passed over.** Not addressed. **This note states no pay rule (G4)**,
   and nothing goes into [../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md](../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md).
9. **Does the yes/no record from the 2026-11-03 Yes still stand?** He did not withdraw it and did not
   mention it. The rest of (f) stays open with it: time of offer, which dispatcher, someone else assigned
   with nothing marked.
10. **Any limit on how long the right lasts.** *"three days"* reads as an example, but he gives no outer
    limit. See also the Friday-to-Sunday difference below.
11. **The form beyond a reminder.** *"automatic reminder"* does not describe a block or a required action
    before saving, and does not rule them out. Whether it appears only on a premium departure is also not
    stated, and depends on item 2.
12. **A rigger.** He excludes *"third men or trucking"*. A rigger is not named. In the vault a third man
    is the driver of an additional load, and those rows are typed *"3rd man or rigger"*
    ([../dispatch/crane-configuration-rules.md](../dispatch/crane-configuration-rules.md) §3.4, `reference`).

## Why

Kyle gives no reason beyond *"the simplest way to follow this"*. The reasons on record remain those of the
2026-04-09 note: the judgement changes with whoever is dispatching, a miss is expensive, and a recorded
refusal is evidence that the crew was offered the work.
