# Decision

**The "paid straight through" (PST) practice will not be built into the ERP — not under that
name, and not under different verbiage.** No token, flag, checkbox, note field, comment code or
pay component may encode "all lunches marked taken, no times recorded, no customer signature
obtained, no work-through-lunch penalty applied, paid start to end."

## What PST is, in the payroll manager's words

Corrina Bustos, transcript at **9:43–10:23** (the employee, not the office, elects it, day by
day):

> The employee does. […] Yes, because it can be, it's a day by day case. […] So one day they can
> choose to do the paid straight through option and another day they can actually get the customer
> signature for the work through lunch. So it's not like an all or nothing. per week, it's a
> day-by-day case.
> And right now, the way that they would qualify for the paid straight through is if they've
> marked that they've taken every lunch. […] showing that they still took their lunch, but they
> don't have times. If they are actually taking a lunch, they will obviously mark the lunch
> completed and write the time in there.

Corrina at **7:41**, on why the practice is already unnamed in FCC:

> because we can't put… any code words or letters or like internally we call it PST, paid straight
> through, but we can't have that on there because it could be flagged. Because it's not. It's not
> something that like. It's kind of like finding the loophole in the union system. So we don't want
> to obviously call attention to it.

Corrina at **11:06**, when asked to build it into the new system:

> We would need we would need different verbiage because we can't have that out there in case
> there's an audit.

That request — *build it under different verbiage* — is what this decision refuses.

# The two lawful outcomes the build must support

Brian Petros stated the rule PST substitutes for, **34:20**:

> I just wanted to note a couple things, like one thing about paying straight through. Like if we're
> paying them straight through, basically saying, okay, you were working the whole time, it may pay
> them at standard rate or an overtime rate or double time rate. But the union rule states that when
> they work through their lunch, that 30 minute period must be a double time, unless it's a holiday,
> then it's triple time. So there's a difference by of just the system paying them straight through
> or applying the double time to it, you know.

**Paying "straight through" at the running rate is therefore not equivalent to applying the rule.**
The half hour is priced at DT (x3 on a holiday) regardless of the bucket the rest of the shift is
in — which is exactly why the *when* of the lunch is load-bearing (Brian at 19:03 and 40:58: the
step-by-step prompt on the work ticket is *"essential"*).

And the WTL self-signature path, which stays — Corrina Bustos, **12:59**:

> Yeah, so right now, if if they're the customer, like, because some jobs, there's like, there's
> just no foreman on site. So they will have. They're okay to self-sign, but they have to mark why
> they are doing it, not just because they didn't want to get the customer signature. If they have to
> say like, customer not on site, or if they were driving during the time that they were supposed to
> be working, obviously the customer's not in the truck with them. So those are the only times that
> it's okay for them. to self-signature, they shouldn't ever just not get it signed. And because if
> so, then they don't qualify for it. And that's when we have to reach out and have a conversation
> with them.

So a self-signed WTL is valid **only with a stated reason** (customer not on site; driving during
the meal window). An unexplained self-sign disqualifies the WTL and triggers a conversation with
the employee. This is `fr-fop-015`'s customer-unavailable fallback with the client's conditions
attached, and it is **not** PST: the penalty still applies.

# What this decision does NOT decide

Four things were explicitly left open on the same call. None of them may be treated as settled by
this note.

1. **The field-side practice itself.** Melissa McNutt, **18:36**: *"I think before we make a
   decision, we need to get Kyle on the call and in a loop because he's been having a really hard
   time with this with the field guys for a while now. So I don't want to make a decision without
   him."* Kyle Harlow, **36:06**, once on the call: *"I think that there is probably something we can
   talk about probably off this call that we can come up with maybe a solution that will work. Like
   kind of what our solution is right now is that They're being overpaid. And that is what is that is
   where it's at, in my mind, in the talking to the lawyer, that box is what we need. But maybe
   that's not enough."* The decision above binds **the ERP**; how the field stops the practice, or
   is paid lawfully instead, is off-call work owned by Melissa McNutt and Kyle Harlow.
2. **The union's answer.** Brian Petros, **40:38–41:50**: *"I've spent a lot of time working with
   these rules. I'm familiar with the contract language. I honestly think that this is much simpler
   than we're making it out to be… It requires a little bit of clarification from the union. I've
   sent questions that we can pose to the union based on the responses to those to clarify, then it
   would make it even simpler… and comply with all the rules and everything, and even lower the cost
   if we get clarification from the union… And having the step-by-step process on the work ticket and
   the ERP is essential."* **Responses were pending at the time of the call.**
3. **California law over the union.** Wes Staley, **42:03**: *"Adding to that, Brian, we would have
   to get clarification from our labor law attorney because California law supersedes the union. Even
   if they tell you you can jump off a cliff, you can't do that."* And **42:35**: *"we probably need
   to get everybody in the same room and figure this out on how we can get paths past this and not be
   in a gray area."* So a favourable union answer alone does not unblock anything.
4. **The lunch threshold values and three-versus-four lunches.** Untouched by this call.
