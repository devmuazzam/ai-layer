# Last-crew entitlement, follow-up examples: Crew A keeps the day, assigned crew first, the yard-departure time, holidays

*`verified` here means that Kyle Harlow wrote this reply. It has not been checked against the union
agreement, and it settles only what is listed under* What this changes. *It adds to the 2026-11-05
note and supersedes nothing. Every item numbered (1)–(12) below is an open item of that note's*
What was not decided *list.*

## Decision

In Kyle Harlow's words, what the reply settles:

1. **The crew on the last straight shift is entitled, and the premium second shift is theirs.**
   *"Crew a would be entitled to the whole day. The second shift It is premium time so the crew is
   entitled to it."* As recorded, the example had Crew A on the last straight shift and Crew B then
   on premium. On the plain reading, *"the crew"* in his second sentence is Crew A. That puts the right
   with the last straight-shift crew, not with *"whoever was on the machine last"* (item 1).
2. **On a straight-shift departure the union agreement lets anyone go; company policy puts the
   assigned crew first.** *"According to the rules of local agreement, we can put anyone on it, but
   it's our company policy that we keep the assigned crew on their assigned machines if available
   always first. If there's not a local 12 rule being broken by doing that."* This is two separate
   things. One is the union entitlement, which is premium-only (2026-11-05). The other is a company
   priority for the assigned crew, which applies *"always"*. Where the two conflict, the Local 12 rule
   wins.
3. **The test is when the crane leaves the yard.** *"If the crane leaves at 4:30 PM that it's
   considered leaving the yard on straight time."* The example was a 5:30 PM job. A crane that leaves
   on straight time is a straight-shift departure, even though the job starts in premium hours.
4. **Local 12 holidays are premium, the same as Saturday and Sunday.** *"Holidays that are recognized
   by Local 12 listed in our book. Are considering premium time … So yes, holidays are considered
   premium time just like Saturdays and Sundays."*
5. **He also states the holiday pay.** *"they pay at a rate of a guaranteed eight hours triple
   time."* This is the same as the contract and the payroll register (see *Compared*). This note
   creates no pay rule and changes none (G4).

## What this changes

Each line is set against the 2026-11-05 note's open items (1)–(12):

- **(1) Which crew: moved, settled for the case as asked.** Crew A, the last straight-shift crew, holds
  the right, and the premium shift after it is theirs. Crew B's later premium run does not give Crew B
  the right. That matches *"the crew that was on the crane on the last straight shift"* (2026-11-05).
  Read that way, *"whoever was on the machine last"* in the same 11-05 reply was the assigned-crew
  sentence, now explained by (2). **Still open:** if Crew A declined the premium shift, or did not
  respond, and Crew B worked it, does Crew A keep the right to the next premium departure?
- **(2) Assigned versus not assigned: moved in part.** The 2026-11-05 tension is explained. The union
  entitlement applies only to a premium departure and goes to the last straight-shift crew. Assigned
  crew first is **company policy**, it applies whenever the assigned crew is available, and it gives
  way to a Local 12 rule. **Still open:** what makes a crew *"assigned"* to a machine (still open in
  [../dispatch/gaps.md](../dispatch/gaps.md) → *Default crew per unit, overridable per job*). Also open:
  what *"if available"* means, and whether the assigned crew is offered the work (and may refuse it) or
  is simply put on it.
- **(3) The boundaries: moved in part.** Leaving the yard is the moment tested. A crane that leaves on
  straight time is a straight-shift departure, even when the job starts in premium hours. That answers
  the run-into-premium question **for the departure**: what counts is when the crane leaves, not when
  the work runs. **Still open:** a departure at exactly 5:00 PM or exactly 6:00 AM. Also open: whether
  the system tests the scheduled leave-yard time on the DAS or the actual departure.
- **(4) Holidays: moved, settled.** Local 12 holidays are premium, as Saturday and Sunday are.
  **Still open:** whether a holiday counts as premium all day, the way Saturday and Sunday do (implied
  by *"just like"*, not stated). Also open: whether the observed day counts, since the contract moves a
  Sunday holiday to Monday and a Saturday Christmas or New Year's Day to the Friday before.
- **(10) How long the right lasts: a hint, not settled.** *"the whole day"* says the right covers the
  rest of the day of the last straight shift. It gives no outer limit. It does not speak to the
  2026-11-05 case of a machine that *"sat for three days"*, or to Friday into Sunday. Reading *"whole
  day"* as the limit would contradict that case, so this note does not read it that way.
- **(11) When the reminder appears: touched, not settled.** Item (11) depended on item (2). With the
  union entitlement now premium-only, the last-crew reminder has something to apply to only on a
  premium departure. Whether the company-policy assigned-crew priority should get any prompt in the
  system on straight departures is new and open (see below).
- **(5), (6), (7), (8), (9), (12): not addressed.** The holiday pay sentence is about the holiday rate.
  It is not item (8), the pay owed when the crew is passed over.

## What was not decided

Nothing below is filled in here. Each item is written so that a short reply settles it.

1. **Crew A passed or silent.** If Crew A declined the premium second shift or did not respond, and
   Crew B worked it, who is called first for the next premium departure: Crew A or Crew B?
2. **What makes a crew "assigned".** Who sets it, and does it pass to someone else? Also: what does
   *"if available"* mean? Is the assigned crew offered the work, or put on it?
3. **The company-policy priority in the system.** Should the DAS show or rank the assigned crew first
   on a straight-shift departure, as it reminds dispatch of the last crew on a premium one? Or is it
   left to dispatch?
4. **A departure at exactly 5:00 PM or 6:00 AM**: premium or straight?
5. **Scheduled or actual.** Is the test the leave-yard time scheduled on the DAS or the actual
   departure? This matters when a crane is scheduled at 4:45 PM and leaves at 5:10 PM.
6. **A crane that does not leave the yard.** A crane held on site overnight, or moved job to job, has
   no yard departure. Which moment is tested then?
7. **Holiday day and date.** Is a holiday premium all day? Does the observed Monday or Friday count, or
   the calendar date?
8. Still open from the 2026-11-05 list and not touched by this reply: (5) *"timely manner"* on
   non-emergency work, and what counts as an emergency. (6) Who sends the text and makes the call.
   (7) Who fills the other seat. (8) The pay owed when the crew is passed over (G4). (9) Whether the
   2026-11-03 yes/no record stands, and the rest of (f). (10) An outer limit on the right. (11) The form
   beyond a reminder. (12) A rigger.

### Holiday pay: agrees, no rule changed

*"a guaranteed eight hours triple time"* matches:

- MLA Art. III *Holidays* §1, *"A minimum of eight (8) hours at triple (3) time"*, over nine listed
  holidays
  ([../payroll/mcog-l12-mla-2025-2028/01-article-iii-working-rules.md](../payroll/mcog-l12-mla-2025-2028/01-article-iii-working-rules.md),
  `unverified`).
- [../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md](../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md), the PAY-032/033 row:
  *Holiday ×3, 8-hour minimum*, **Confirmed + Contract**.
- [../payroll/source-attribution-and-conflicts.md](../payroll/source-attribution-and-conflicts.md): MLA
  *"9 holidays at ×3 min 8 h"*.

Kyle does not mention §4.1 of the same article. It says that on non-construction work, with a union
permit, holidays may be worked at double time. *"just like Saturdays and Sundays"* is read here as
being premium, not as being paid the same. Under Art. III *Shifts* ¶1, overtime is at least ×1.5 and
Sundays are ×2. The yard
departure test decides **who is called**. It does not say how the hours are paid, and this note does
not carry it into payroll. Nothing goes into the provenance register (G4).

## Why

Kyle gives a reason only for scenario two. The open-to-anyone rule is the union agreement's, and the
assigned-crew priority is *"our company policy"*, kept *"always first"* unless a Local 12 rule would be
broken. The reasons on record for managing the entitlement in the system are still those of the
2026-04-09 note: the judgement changes with whoever is dispatching, a miss is expensive, and a recorded
refusal is evidence that the crew was offered the work.
