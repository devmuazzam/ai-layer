# Last-crew entitlement is managed in the system; offers and refusals are recorded

*Decided 2026-04-09. Written 2026-10-23, six months later, once the exchange was found while
filing the 2026-10-22 gap below.*

## Decision

Kyle Harlow, 2026-04-09, on two points:

1. **The last-crew entitlement is managed in the system, not manually.** When a crew has a right to
   the next shift on its crane, the ERP is to tell dispatch so, so that whoever is dispatching knows
   *"I have to call that crew first"*. It is not left to the dispatcher's memory or judgement.
2. **Dispatch records which entitled crew members accepted or refused the offer** (agreed in
   principle). The record is kept in the system as evidence that the crew was offered the work.

This note settles **how the entitlement is handled**: in the system, with a record of offers and
refusals. It does **not** settle **what the entitlement is**. When it applies, who holds it, what
is owed if it is missed, and what DAS does are all open (see *What was not decided*).

## Why

Kyle's reasons, 1:18:56:

- **The judgement changes with the person.** When Lisette (Lisette Anzaldo, Dispatch Manager) is
  away, *"somebody else decides to make a judgment"*.
- **A miss is expensive.** He calls the consequence *"those charges"*, which *"can be extremely
  expensive for the company"*. This note does not say what the charges are (see G4 below).
- **The ERP exists to remove that kind of human error.** *"the whole point of this system is to
  remove as much of that as possible"*.

The reason for recording offers and refusals is ehtesham.anwar's, and Kyle agreed with it. A refusal
on record is evidence, a *"data point that we can later on show to the relevant authorities"*, that
the entitled crew was offered the work.

## What was not decided

All of this stays open in [../dispatch/gaps.md](../dispatch/gaps.md) → *First right of refusal on
the next day's crane — last-crew entitlement*.

- **(a) The trigger is contested.** Melissa McNutt's e-mail of 2026-04-08
  ([../erp-review-log.md](../erp-review-log.md) #4, `unverified`): *"The last crew on the equipment on
  **straight time** is entitled to the overtime work"*. Her example crew ends at 11:00. Kyle, at
  1:17:12 in this same meeting (*"who was last on premium time"*) and again on 2026-10-22: the crew
  **ended on overtime/premium time**, and on 10-22 only when the crane restarts before 6 AM. When
  ehtesham.anwar restated the rule at 1:18:10, he did not state the trigger, and Kyle's
  *"that's what I was just speaking"* (1:18:23) does not settle it. Nobody chose between the two.
- **(b) What counts as next-shift overtime.** Before 06:00, after 17:00, a Saturday after a Friday shift
  (Melissa: Friday carries to Saturday, not to Sunday), or all of these.
- **(c) Who holds the right.** The whole crew on the crane (Melissa's *"Bob and Tom"*), and whether it
  extends to a driver or a rigger.
- **(d) The pay owed when the crew is passed over. Not decided, and this note states no pay rule.**
  The sources describe a consequence: *"we could be liable to pay all 4 employees"* (Melissa,
  2026-04-08), and *"they are entitled to get another pay for that ship even though they did not
  work"* (ehtesham.anwar's restatement, 1:18:26, to which Kyle said *"It. Yes."*). Neither is a rule
  anyone adopted for the ERP. Whether the ERP computes it, and what amount, is open. If it is ever
  computed, it needs its own row, with its source, in
  [../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md](../payroll/payroll-rules-provenance.md) (guardrail G4)
  before any code. Kyle's *"in the system"* was about knowing whom to call first. It was not about
  computing pay.
- **(e) The form in DAS.** *"In the system"* is decided. Whether DAS ranks the entitled crew first
  (ehtesham.anwar suggested folding it into the *"preferred"* idea, 1:19:46), warns when another crew is
  dropped, or blocks until an offer is recorded is open. Melissa's e-mail asks that *"the system
  notifies dispatch that an action item is required before scheduling"*, but nobody chose between
  these forms.
- **(f) The detail of the record.** Recording accepted and refused offers is agreed in principle.
  Open: where the record lives (DAS card, ticket, employee), who enters it (*"the dispatch manager"*
  in the proposal, or any dispatcher), and what it holds (time of offer, channel, who offered).
- **How the crew is reached.** Brian Petros (Data Analyst, ERP Project Coordinator; per the transcript
  label) asked about sending text notifications to operators and oilers and collecting
  accept or reject replies (1:20:49, 1:21:25). Kyle doubted that a system notification wakes anyone
  at midnight (*"I was blowing their phone up time and time and time … we could try to make it
  work"*, 1:21:25). A 15-minute answer window was also mentioned (about 1:23:33). None of this was
  decided, and this note does not say that offers are made through the system. It says only that
  they are recorded there.
