## What the answers say, in short

This is a reading of the answers, kept close to her words. It does not add a rule.

**Holdover job (A).**

- **Day 1.** The oiler does the pre-trip **at the yard** (A2) and takes the crane to site (A1). On
  site, before build-up, the crew does the JSA and the pre-lift tailboard meeting (A3). At the end of
  the day the crane **typically stays built** in place (A4).
- **Day 1, deferred to Kyle Harlow.** Whether a procedure is needed before leaving the crane on site
  (A4). Whether a post-trip is required at the end of Day 1 (A5: *"Kyle Harlow , Yes?"*). Both are
  questions to Kyle. Neither is an answer.
- **Days 2+.** The crew reports **directly to the job site**, about 99% of the time in a personal
  car. A company truck is a one-off when the job is far (A6). Time starts when they **arrive at the
  job site** (A7). The first activity is a **pre-trip / daily inspection**, plus a JSA (A8, A9).
  There is no repeated build-up or breakdown (A10).
- **Last day.** The crane is broken down on the last day only. *"The end of that day is when they
  leave the site"*, and *"the end of the job will be when the equipment returns back to our yard"*
  (A11). There is a post-trip, then breakdown, then the return to the yard (A12).

**Late and incomplete tickets (B).**

- **The window.** Every field employee may complete or edit their own ticket, **all steps**, with
  the actual times for the original date, for **24 hours from "their original ticket time"** (B1, B2,
  and the final confirmation). TCG wants paperwork done the same day. The same deadline applies to
  everything (B2).
- **Inspections.** *"We need to make the workflow so they can't skip an inspection"* (B1). A late
  inspection submission needs **no approval** (B4).
- **Reminders.** *"Maybe when they leave the yard, 12 hours later and then a final reminder at 20
  hours"* (B2).
- **After 24 hours.** Approval from a **manager or payroll** is needed. The worker requests it in the
  app, the approver gives it with one click, and the worker then has **4 hours** to complete (B2).
  Managers and admins can override (B3).
- **Who.** Operators, Oilers, 3rd men, riggers, mechanics and yard techs complete their own tickets.
  To *"supervisors/dispatchers also … on their behalf?"* she answered *"Yes"* (B3).
- **After submission.** No edit without approval from a manager or payroll (B4: *"shouldn't be
  edited with our approval"* — read as *without*). An already approved ticket needs an edit request
  to the manager or payroll (B6). An invoiced or payroll-processed ticket needs approval. A change
  after billing triggers re-approval (B6: *"yes!"*). **Payroll can edit at all times** (final
  confirmation).
- **Visibility and audit.** A late completion is marked, internally only, and never on the
  customer's paperwork (B4). There is a full audit history: who, when, what, original value and new
  value (B5).
- **Notifications and reporting.** Managers and payroll are notified when an old or incomplete
  ticket is completed or modified (B5). An incomplete or overdue report goes to managers and payroll
  (B6).
- **Offline.** Forms should save on the tablet and sync when service returns, *"the same thing"* as
  FCC today. This was given as the answer to the *employee leaves the company* question (B6).
