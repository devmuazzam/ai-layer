# What a finished lift plan package contains — two worked samples

**What this is.** Two complete, real lift-plan submittal packages, delivered as single PDFs. They
are the **output** of the process in
[lift-plan-request-and-3d-lift-plan.md](lift-plan-request-and-3d-lift-plan.md), and they answer a
question no other document in the drop answers: *what documents does a lift plan actually consist
of, and where does each come from?* This page records the **anatomy and field inventory**. The
customer-specific values (loads, radii, weights, named individuals, certificate numbers) were not
transcribed.

| | Sample A | Sample B |

| Pages | 56 (41 with a text layer) | 55 (40 with a text layer) |

| Prepared by | A named TCG estimator, with direct phone and email on the cover | — |

**A package is a bound set of sectioned documents, with divider pages carrying only a section
name.** Sample A's dividers read *Lift Detail*, *Crane Specifications*, *Crane Certifications*,
*Operators*, *Rigging Equipment*, *COI*, *Standards*. Sample B's read *Site Logistics*,
*Lift Detail*, *Crane Specifications*, *Crane Certifications*, *Operator Certifications*,
*Standards*.

## The section anatomy

### 1. Cover and site logistics

Cover page: package title, customer, project. TCG estimator name, email and phone. Sample B adds a
**Site Logistics** section (a divider plus an image-only page — no text layer, so its content is
unknown).

### 2. Lift Detail — the Lift Evaluation Form

The heart of the package. In Sample A this is a **customer-mandated form**: the LAUSD
*Lift Evaluation Form*, submitted to `construction_safety@laschools.org`. Header fields: submitted
by, date, company, address, phone, fax, email; school name, project name, lift location, address,
date of lift, contractor; and a free-text **description of load, dimensions and weight**.

Then six numbered sections:

**1 CRANE** — type of crane · manufacturer · model no. · serial no. · crane capacity ·
**Load Moment Indicator fitted? · Anti-Two-Block device fitted? · load chart in the crane? ·
operator's handbook in the crane?** · lifting arrangement (on boom / on jib), and then, from the
load chart: maximum operating radius · loaded boom length · loaded boom angle at pick-up · loaded
boom angle at set · rated capacity over rear / over front / over side · **crane's rated capacity for
this lift** · jib length and angle · **percentage of the crane's rated capacity** (59% in Sample A).

**2 RIGGING COMPONENTS** — *sling selection*: type, size, length, number of legs, weight per foot,
total weight, angle, capacity, **are qualified riggers used?**, how the rigging attaches to the load.
*Shackle selection*: pin diameter, capacity, number, weight per shackle, total weight, and how the
shackle attaches (factory eye, field-welded eye, etc.).

**3 LOAD LINE** — cable size · weight per foot · number of parts · distance from boom/jib head to the
load attachment point · weight of the load fall.

**4 WEIGHT** — headache ball · block · lifting/spreader bar · jib weight (or the load-chart deduction
if stowed) · allowance for other weight · **weight of the load** · **total weight** ·
**source of the load-weight information** (name plate, drawings, scaled, calculated) ·
**weights verified by — name and qualifications**.

**5 CRANE PLACEMENT** — outriggers fully extended? (attach the applicable load chart and indicate the
data used) · surface the crane sits on · **will the surface bearing capacity support crane and
load?** · **how bearing capacity was determined** · are trench plates, mats or cribbing required, and
what type and size · obstructions to lift or swing · swing direction, degree and distance ·
**will the entire counterweight swing radius be barricaded, and with what?**

**6 LIFT PLANNING** — qualified signalman used? · **who is the responsible person for the lift?** ·
occupied facilities within the swing radius? · tag lines? · **what time will the lift take place?** ·
**when and where the lift tailgate meeting will be held** · **wind speed at which crane work is
stopped** (20 mph in Sample A) · radios needed? · emergency procedures developed and implemented? ·
**traffic control plan developed and implemented?** · qualified traffic flaggers? ·
**list any known hazards** — electrical, overhead, underground — including the crane's path of travel
for underground utilities if tracking the crane.

### 3. Job Safety Analysis (JSA)

TCG's own two-page JSA form. Header: crane used, project location, **qualifications needed to
complete the work** (Sample A: "NCCO and Qualified Rigger/signalman"). Then a **hazard-identification
grid of 24 checkbox risks** — falling objects, electrical >4001 amps, flammable materials in area,
elevated work area, poor lighting, manual handling, pressurized fluids, communication difficulties,
slippery surfaces, sharp materials, suspended loads, moving machinery, multiple electrical feeds,
ladders used, poor visibility, tools and equipment, trip hazards, working at heights, inhalable
dust/fibres, shackles/chains/slings, electrical hazards, working near cranes, high noise areas,
radiation.

A **Precautions** checkbox list — gloves, goggles, full face shield, high-visibility vest, fall
arrest systems, fire extinguishers, harness, erect warning signs, tag lines, barricades — sits beside
a **Permits** checkbox list of exactly three: **Street Permits · Lane Closures · FAA**.

> That three-item permit checklist is the field-level counterpart to everything in
> [../city-permits/](../city-permits/INDEX.md), and it is what the client's request to "replace the
> scattered permit checkboxes with an expandable checklist covering travel permits, lift plan, safety
> and FAA" ([`../../erp-review-log.md`](../../erp-review-log.md)) is reacting to.

The JSA then carries a prose **General Safety Overview** with control measures grouped by hazard:
dropped loads · uneven surfaces / slips and sprains · environmental conditions (sunlight, heat, poor
lighting, wind) · crush injuries · overhead power lines · site and work inductions · outrigger pad
placement · overturning due to unstable conditions · oil leaks and fuel spills. It also covers
**counterweight trucks** and **dolly trailers**.

Sample B carries a different, more formal safety document instead: a **Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)**
for the activity "CRANE OPERATION", with an overall **Risk Assessment Code (RAC)** using the highest
code among its rows, step-by-step hazards and controls (starting and warming up · unanticipated
rolling or movement · review the operator's manual if new to the machine), and an "Additional Safety
Considerations" list including an equipment checklist and stowing requirements.

### 4. Engineering output from the 3D Lift Plan software

- **Unit Report** per load (Sample A has two: "AC 1" and "AC 2"), each stamped with project name,
  date, preparer and time.
- **Certified Drawing** per load — two pages each, largely image.
- **Crane configuration pages** — one per crane/configuration modelled, headed with manufacturer,
  model and boom configuration (Sample B carries six such pages for its three candidate cranes).
- **Rigging diagram** — Sample A: "6' Spreader Bar / 10' Slings / 6.5 ton".
- **Load Chart** — project, customer, and the chart itself.
- **Ground Bearing Pressure Below Crane Mats** — a job-information block plus the calculation.
  Sample B additionally carries two **Link-Belt Pontoon Loading Program** outputs
  (`groundbearing.linkbelt.com`, Link-Belt Cranes, Lexington, Kentucky), timestamped.

### 5. Crane specifications

Manufacturer technical-data sheets — Sample B's carry the Link-Belt document number
`5424 (supersedes 5411)---1004---N3`, with *Technical Data* and *General Dimensions* pages.

### 6. Crane certifications

Four **State of California** crane certificates per package, issued by **American Crane
Certification # 413** (Plate V, 4906 Willowbrook Drive). Each is one page.

### 7. Operator certifications

One page per operator. Sample A: two operators, one **NCCCO Certified** (issued 04.30.2024) and one
**CCO Certified** (issued 05.31.2022). Sample B: four operators — one **OECP Certified** (expires
01.15.2027) and three CCO/NCCCO Certified (issued 4.30.2021, 05.31.2022, 05.31.2020).
**Some records show an issue date and some an expiry date; the packages are not consistent.**

### 8. Rigging equipment

Manufacturer capacity tables: **wire rope · round slings (endless, ENR) · chains · alloy anchor
shackles (working load limit 2 to 120 tons)**.

### 9. Certificate of insurance

Sample A carries a full **ACORD Certificate of Liability Insurance** dated 12/31/2025 plus an
**Additional Remarks Schedule** (agency customer ID `CRANGUY-01`). Four further pages carry only
policy-reference numbers (`SCR0000526-05`, `ORANGL000819-03`) and "Page intentionally left blank"
markers — endorsement forms that did not convert.

### 10. Standards

A divider page, then, in Sample B, the **customer's own drawings**: a EUSERC utility one-line for
Southern California Edison, a six-section switchgear elevation, and two pages of general notes
(power system data — 16500 V 3-phase 3-wire 60 Hz solidly grounded; shorting terminal blocks for all
CT circuits; strip heaters with thermostat). Sample A's Standards section did not convert.

## What this tells you that the product model does not

`projects-jobs/project-module/04-create-and-approve-lift-plans.md` models a lift plan as a **form
with attachments**. These samples show it is really a **bound multi-source package**: a customer- or
authority-mandated evaluation form, TCG's own JSA, software output, manufacturer datasheets, and
**four categories of expiring credential** — crane certifications, operator certifications, the
rigging capacity references, and the certificate of insurance.

Three consequences the samples demonstrate but no document states as a requirement:

1. **A lift plan can carry more than one candidate crane.** Sample B models three, at the same boom
   configuration. The existing vault page requires each plan to be "linked to a specific
   crane/resource"; this package is not.
2. **The certificates inside a package expire.** An operator certificate in Sample B expires
   01.15.2027; the COI in Sample A expired 12/31/2025. A stored package is only valid while its
   attachments are.
3. **The evaluation form may be the customer's, not TCG's.** Sample A's is LAUSD's, submitted to a
   school-district safety mailbox. That is a per-customer document requirement the product model
   has no place for.

**None of the three is stated as an ERP requirement anywhere in this drop.**
