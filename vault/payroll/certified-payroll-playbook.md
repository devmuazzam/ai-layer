**The first process documentation for certified payroll in the record** — a Sales-Coordinator
playbook. Key structural fact: certified payroll starts at **customer intake (sales)**, gated
by a **Labor Compliance** function, *before* a job may be scheduled. Transcribed faithfully
from the photographed pages.

## 1. Purpose
Helps Sales Coordinators recognize when Certified Payroll is required, collect the correct
information during customer intake, and ensure Labor Compliance has everything needed before
the job is scheduled.

## 2. What is Certified Payroll?
A weekly payroll report required on many prevailing-wage and public-works projects. It
verifies employees were paid the correct prevailing wage, fringe benefits, and
classifications for the work performed.

## 3. When required
- Many California public-works projects
- Many Davis-Bacon Act (federally funded) projects
- When the contract, bid package, or customer specifically requires it

## 4. How to identify a Certified Payroll job
| Look for | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Contract language | Certified Payroll, CPR, **WH-347**, Prevailing Wage |
| Public agencies | LAUSD, Caltrans, Metro, LADWP, cities, counties |
| Labor documents | **DIR Project ID**, Wage Determination, Labor Compliance Packet |
| Customer request | Weekly payroll reporting or labor-compliance requirements |

## 5. Sales Coordinator checklist
- Ask if Certified Payroll is required
- Determine if the project is public works or federally funded
- Obtain the labor compliance packet if available
- Send the Labor Compliance Form to the customer
- **Mark Labor compliance in FCC so it populates to labor compliance** *(≈ the FCC employee/job
  "Certified Pay" flag seen 2026-06-09)*
- Customer sends the form directly back to Labor Compliance (forward it if received)

## 6. Go / No-Go decision
**GO:** requirements confirmed, Labor Compliance notified, documents attached.
**NO-GO:** if Certified Payroll is required — **or you are unsure** — do not schedule the job
until Labor Compliance reviews the project.

## 7. Common red flags
School district or government owner · prevailing-wage language · DIR registration references ·
federal funding/grant references · requests for weekly payroll reports.

## 8. Best practices
Never assume it is not required · if the customer says "I'm not sure," mark the job
**Unknown** and escalate · keep all labor-compliance documents with the job record · ask
early — before the job reaches Dispatch.

## 9. Success standard
Every potential Certified Payroll project is identified during customer intake, reviewed by
Labor Compliance when required, and documented **before the job enters the "Ready to Schedule
Value Engine"** *(TCG/FCC pipeline term — undefined anywhere in the record; glossary candidate)*.

---

**ERP consequences:** the intake side (checklist, red flags, Unknown-and-escalate, the
scheduling gate, document custody on the job record) belongs to **quotations/jobs**; the
reporting side (weekly WH-347-class output, wage determinations, classifications) is
[PAY-072](rules/PAY-072-certified-payroll-davis-bacon-reporting.md)'s scope. "Labor
Compliance" is a named organizational function — the module finally has a business owner
candidate.

## 10. Companion playbook — Davis-Bacon Act & federally funded jobs

**Source:** `3.1.1_MODULE-CRM-SALES-QUOTES/__ERP_Sales Process (from Ryan 2026-08-06)/The_Crane_Guys_Playbook_-_Davis_Bacon_and_Federally_Funded_Jobs.docx`.
Same nine-section template, same audience, same Go/No-Go shape. It sits **upstream** of the
certified-payroll playbook: this one asks *is this job federally funded or public works at all?*,
the other asks *does it need weekly CPRs?*

**Purpose.** Help Sales Coordinators identify Davis-Bacon, federally funded and California public
works projects **before a job is scheduled** — *"prevents delays, compliance issues, and unexpected
costs."*

**Definitions, as written.** The **Davis-Bacon Act** is *"a federal law requiring contractors and
subcontractors on certain federally funded or federally assisted construction projects to pay
workers at least the applicable prevailing wage and comply with federal labor reporting
requirements."* A **federally funded job** is *"any project that receives federal money, whether
fully or partially, through grants, contracts, or other federal assistance. **Not every public
project is federally funded, so always verify the funding source.**"* On California: public works
projects *"often require prevailing wages, DIR registration, and may require certified payroll.
**Federal and California requirements can both apply on the same project.**"*

**How to spot one:**

| Look for | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Public agency owner | LAUSD, Caltrans, LADWP, City, County, Metro, **Port** |
| Contract language | Davis-Bacon, Prevailing Wage, Certified Payroll, DIR |
| **Federal agencies** | **USACE, NAVFAC, FHWA, FAA, FEMA, HUD, DOE** |
| Bid documents | Wage determination, labor compliance packet, WH-347 references |

The federal-agency row is the one thing here that the certified-payroll playbook does not have —
a named list of the federal funders whose presence triggers Davis-Bacon.

**Screening questions.** Is the owner a government or public agency? · Is the project federally
funded or grant funded? · Does the contract mention Davis-Bacon, prevailing wage, DIR, or certified
payroll? · Is there a labor compliance packet or wage determination? · Has Labor Compliance
reviewed the project if required?

**Go / No-Go.** *GO:* all labor compliance requirements identified and documented — *"Proceed to
the Ready to Schedule Value Engine."* *NO-GO:* **if the funding source or labor requirements are
unknown**, stop the scheduling process and escalate to Labor Compliance. Note the asymmetry with
the certified-payroll playbook: there, *unsure* triggers no-go; here, *unknown funding* does. Both
resolve to the same instruction — *"mark the job as 'Unknown' and send it to Labor Compliance."*

**Red flags:** government agency customer · school district project · **infrastructure or utility
work** · references to prevailing wage or certified payroll · **funding source marked 'Federal',
'Grant', or 'BIL'**.

**Success standard.** *"Every project must be screened for labor compliance before scheduling."*
Not *every potential certified-payroll project* — **every project**. This playbook makes labor-
compliance screening a universal gate on the quote-to-schedule path, which is a stronger claim
than anything else in the record and is a requirement on the quotations/jobs module, not payroll.

## 11. The form the checklist refers to — Pre-Job Labor Compliance & Payroll Information Request

**Source:** `1.0.1_BUSINESS-REQUIREMENTS/Customer Packet and Labor Compliance Forms/Pre-Job_Labor_Compliance_Information_Request_Form_final_polished[48].pdf`
(a byte-identical copy also sits at `TCG_FORMS_(paper_and_electronic)/from Melissa 2026-04-02/`,
which dates it to **2026-04-02 or earlier** — well before the playbooks that reference it).

This is the *"Send Labor Compliance Form to Customer"* item in §5's checklist, and it is the
authoritative list of what Labor Compliance needs. Its own gating rules:

- *"This form does **NOT** need to be completed unless the job includes **public works, certified
  payroll reporting, apprenticeship reporting, OCCIP/CCIP, or Project Labor Agreement (PLA)
  requirements**."*
- *"Please allow a minimum of **5 days** prior to job start date to submit all required
  information."*
- Return address: **`Laborcompliance@craneguys.com`** — the named mailbox for the Labor Compliance
  function.

**The fields, which are the data model for a certified-payroll job:**

| Section | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer & Project** (all required) | Customer Name · Project/Job Name · Job Location (address & city) · **Customer Job or Quote Number** · **Total Contract Amount ($)** · Estimated Start Date · End Date · General Contractor Contact · Payroll/LC Contact |
| **Public works & payroll reporting** | ☐ Certified Payroll Reporting Required · ☐ Prevailing Wage / Public Works Project |
| **Apprenticeship & state reporting** (required 5 days prior) | ☐ **DAS 140** · ☐ Apprentice Utilization Requirements · Apprenticeship Program(s) |
| **City / local agency** | ☐ **City of Los Angeles Form LA-131** · ☐ LAUSD / LACMTA / Other |
| **Project Labor Agreement** | ☐ PLA Applies · Platform Style: ☐ **LPC Tracker** ☐ **DIR** ☐ Other · PLA Administrator/Contact · Union / Signatory Requirements |
| **OCCIP / CCIP** | ☐ Enrollment Required · Program Administrator · Administrator Email · Expected Enrollment Deadline |

Five things here are **new to the vault** and none is mentioned in either playbook:

1. **`DAS 140`** — the California apprenticeship-program notification. It ties the employee-level
   `Apprentice` / `Apprentice %` / **`Registration Number`** fields
   ([fcc-employee-master-reference-data.md](fcc-employee-master-reference-data.md) §7) to a
   reporting obligation, and gives [PAY-016](rules/PAY-016-apprentice-wage-step.md) a downstream
   consumer beyond the wage step.
2. **City of Los Angeles Form LA-131** — a city-specific certified-payroll filing.
3. **PLA "Platform Style": LPC Tracker or DIR** — the *submission portal* is a per-project
   attribute. CPRs do not go to one place; where they go is negotiated per job. Any ERP that models
   certified-payroll output as a single WH-347-shaped export will be wrong for PLA jobs.
4. **OCCIP / CCIP** — owner- or contractor-controlled insurance programs, with their own
   administrator, email and enrollment deadline. Neither playbook mentions insurance enrolment as
   a labor-compliance trigger, and it has no representation in the register at all.
5. **Total Contract Amount** is required — a value Sales holds and payroll does not, needed before
   the job starts.

**Where the flag is captured today, and the problem with it.** The New Customer Packet
(`New Customer Packet 10-23-2025_1[75].pdf`, primarily a customers/accounting document — credit
application, deposit form, preliminary notice) asks **"Prevailing Wage: ☐ Yes ☐ No"** in *two*
places: on the **Deposit Form** (alongside customer, date of job, quote #, sales rep, job address)
and on the **Preliminary Notice Information Request Form** (alongside customer, job #, P.O. #). So
the prevailing-wage question is asked at intake, on paper, twice, on forms owned by two different
departments — and separately as a Y/N on the FCC **employee** record and as `UseCertifiedPayroll`
on the FCC **classification**. **Four levels for one flag, no adjudication.** The TCG-annotated
FCC deck argues it belongs on the **job or job site**
([fcc-employee-master-reference-data.md](fcc-employee-master-reference-data.md) §8). That is the
open question, and it is a design decision for [PAY-072](rules/PAY-072-certified-payroll-davis-bacon-reporting.md)
and the quotations module rather than something any of these documents settles.

## 12. The certified-payroll process, as a flowchart

`TCG_VE_PAYROLL_02_2025-09-12.pdf` renders what §5–§9 describe as a two-lane value engine —
**Notified Before Job Start** and **Notified After Job Start** — with seven numbered SOPs
(`SOP #1` salesperson notification / FCC check → `#2` compliance packet & start-job forms → `#3`
run certified payroll report → `#4` report weekly labor & internal tracker → `#5` generate CPR
report & request check stubs → `#6` close out → `#7` customer non-compliant email request). It adds
one concept this playbook does not name: a **CPR Tracker**, plus a weekly non-performance loop that
repeats until close-out. Full transcription:
[payroll-value-engines.md](payroll-value-engines.md) §3.
