# Contracts and COI SOP — routing customer agreements and insurance requests

**What this is.** Two documents. A full **Standard Operating Procedure** (Department: Sales;
Process Owner: **Sales Manager**; Applies To: all sales team members; Effective Date:
*Immediately*) covering both contracts and certificates of insurance; and a one-page **COI Request
Playbook** by **Ryan Adams, 2026-05-11** covering the mechanics of getting a certificate issued.

**Why it matters.** The vault has nothing on contracts, COIs, endorsements or customer-supplied
terms, and the quote's own T&C assumes an insurance exhibit exists (*"The Crane Guys, LLC will
provide it's standard insurance coverage's, as set forth on Exhibit 'TCG'"* —
[quote-terms-and-rate-card.md](quote-terms-and-rate-card.md) §7). This is the process behind
that. It is also, in effect, **an authority model**: a list of things a salesperson may not do.

> The SOP describes a change of process: *"**The Crane Guys now has an internal program available
> to assist with contract review and processing.** To help complete these requests faster and more
> effectively, all contracts and COI requests received by the sales team should be emailed
> directly to the Sales Manager."* The earlier Lead Workflow diagram routed the same work through
> the **Project Engineer** instead — see
> [sales-operating-system/06-value-engine-flow-and-lead-workflow.md](sales-operating-system/06-value-engine-flow-and-lead-workflow.md)
> §2. Both are in the drop; the SOP is later and self-describes as new.

## 1. What counts as a contract

The SOP's scope list. Anything on it triggers the process:

Master Service Agreements · Subcontract Agreements · Service Agreements · Vendor Agreements ·
**Customer Purchase Orders containing terms and conditions** · Rental Agreements · Terms and
Conditions · Indemnity Agreements · Hold Harmless Agreements · Non-Disclosure Agreements ·
Supplier Agreements · Contractor Agreements · Addendums or amendments · **Customer-requested
changes to The Crane Guys' terms** · *"Any document requiring a company signature, initials,
acknowledgment, or acceptance."*

## 2. What counts as an insurance request

Certificate of Insurance requests · **Additional Insured** · **Waiver of Subrogation** ·
**Primary and Noncontributory** · Completed Operations · Automobile Liability requirements ·
Workers' Compensation requirements · Umbrella or Excess Liability requirements · Special
endorsement requests · Insurance exhibits · Customer insurance forms · **Insurance portal
requests** · Requests for proof of insurance.

## 3. The general policy — what a salesperson must not do

> *"The salesperson should **not independently**: Sign a contract. Initial a contract. Accept
> terms and conditions. **Click an electronic acceptance button.** Approve customer contract
> language. Change contract language. Promise that a contract will be signed. **Create or modify a
> COI.** Promise that a specific insurance endorsement will be approved. Agree to insurance limits
> or requirements. **Send the request to multiple internal people.**"*

And the completion test: *"The request is **not considered approved or complete** until the Sales
Manager returns the completed document or confirms that the matter has been resolved."*

## 4. The contract process — 11 steps

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Review what the customer sent** — a basic review, *"not… a legal review"*. Confirm: document type · whether a signature is requested · whether it relates to a specific job · **customer deadline** · whether the work has been quoted · whether it has been scheduled · whether it refers to attachments. *"If the contract refers to attachments that were not included, ask the customer to provide the missing documents."* |
| 2 | **Download the complete document** — main agreement, exhibits, attachments, insurance requirements, scope of work, PO, T&C, safety requirements, payment requirements, e-signature request, and anything referenced within. *"**Do not send only screenshots or selected pages** when the complete agreement is available."* |
| 3 | **Do not sign or approve anything** — and do not tell the customer the agreement is approved, looks acceptable, will be signed without changes, will have no redlines, has been accepted, or that the job is cleared to proceed |
| 4 | **Gather basic information** — customer name and contact · job/project name · jobsite location · **estimated job date and estimated job value** · **quote number** · PO number · customer deadline · new or existing · one project or ongoing · already scheduled · special instructions. *"Do not delay sending the contract if some information is not yet available. Clearly identify any missing information."* |
| 5 | **Email it to the Sales Manager** — forward the customer's original email with all attachments, prefixed by a structured summary (below) |
| 6 | **Sales Manager processes** — confirm receipt · review the information · **submit the agreement through the internal contract program** · coordinate internal review · identify questions or missing information · **coordinate redlines** · communicate next steps · return the completed document |
| 7 | **Respond to questions** — the Sales Manager may ask about exact scope, job value, date, whether the quote is accepted, one job or many, **special payment terms, retainage, prevailing wage or certified payroll, special insurance requirements**, and *"Whether the customer will consider using The Crane Guys' agreement"* |
| 8 | **Customer communication during review** — a supplied holding reply; *"should not make promises regarding approval, signature, redlines, or completion dates unless confirmed"* |
| 9 | **Completed contract returned** — to the salesperson, or to salesperson and customer together, at the Sales Manager's discretion; salesperson stays copied |
| 10 | **Customer questions or objections** — forward to the Sales Manager. The salesperson must not remove redlines, change approved language, accept revisions, negotiate legal language or agree to revised terms |
| 11 | **Final completion** — a contract is complete **only** when final language is approved, required signatures obtained, the customer has signed where required, **a fully executed copy has been received**, required insurance documents are complete, and the Sales Manager confirms |

**The required email summary block** — nine fields, which is effectively the contract-review
record's schema:

```
Customer:
Project or Job Name:
Jobsite Location:
Estimated Job Date:
Estimated Job Value:
Customer Contact:
Customer Deadline:
Job Already Scheduled: Yes or No
Type of Agreement:
Additional Information or Concerns:
```

**Subject-line format:** `CONTRACT REVIEW – Customer Name – Project Name`
(example: *"CONTRACT REVIEW – ABC Electric – Main Street Substation"*), or
`URGENT CONTRACT REVIEW – Customer Name – Project Name – Deadline`.

### Contracts requiring immediate attention

Fourteen triggers for the URGENT prefix. Several are commercial-risk terms the ERP would want to
flag on a quote or job:

- The job is scheduled **within five business days**
- The customer requires an immediate signature; the work has already started
- The customer is refusing to allow contract changes, or is threatening to award elsewhere
- The agreement contains **retainage** · **liquidated damages** · **pay-if-paid or pay-when-paid**
  language · **bonding requirements** · **unusually long payment terms**
- **The agreement conflicts with The Crane Guys' quote**
- **The purchase order includes terms and conditions**
- The agreement refers to another contract that was not provided
- The agreement requires TCG to accept responsibility for **delays, site conditions, or the work
  of others**

> *"The salesperson does not need to determine the legal impact of these items. The salesperson
> should simply **identify them to the Sales Manager when noticed**."*

## 5. The COI process — 10 steps

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Review the request** — 13 possible request types (§2). *"The salesperson is not expected to determine whether the requested coverage is available."* |
| 2 | **Obtain the requirements in writing** — insurance exhibit, sample COI, written instructions, customer checklist, contract section, portal instructions or email. *"If the request was made verbally, ask the customer to email the exact requirements."* |
| 3 | **Gather basic information** — including **the certificate holder's exact legal name and full address**. *"The salesperson should **not guess** the customer's legal name, address, or required wording."* |
| 4 | **Do not create or modify insurance documents** — do not create, edit, change wording, promise an endorsement, promise acceptance, state coverage without confirmation, sign insurance forms, **provide policy copies without approval**, or agree to limits |
| 5 | **Email the request to the Sales Manager** with the structured block below |
| 6 | **Sales Manager processes** — confirms receipt, reviews, identifies missing information, routes through the proper internal or insurance process, **addresses unusual limits or endorsements**, tracks until complete, returns the documents |
| 7 | **Respond to missing information** — certificate holder name and address, insurance exhibit, endorsement forms, jobsite address, project name, job date, description of work, correct contact |
| 8 | **Customer communication** — supplied holding reply; *"should not promise same-day completion unless the Sales Manager has confirmed it"* |
| 9 | **Completed COI returned** — same two routing options as a contract |
| 10 | **Customer rejects or questions** — forward to the Sales Manager; do not modify the document or promise a correction |

**The required COI email block** — twelve fields:

```
Customer:
Certificate Holder Name:
Certificate Holder Address:
Project or Job Name:
Jobsite Address:
Job Date:
Customer Contact:
Customer Deadline:
Quote or Job Number:
Required Endorsements:
Contract Also Under Review: Yes or No
Special Instructions:
```

**Subject line:** `COI REQUEST – Customer Name – Project Name`, or
`URGENT COI REQUEST – Customer Name – Project Name – Job Date`.

### COI requests requiring immediate attention

Eleven triggers, of which the operationally hard ones are: **the job begins within two business
days** · **the customer will not allow the crew onsite without the COI** · the customer has
rejected a previously issued COI · higher limits or unusual endorsements are required · **actual
policy copies** are requested · a specific endorsement form is required · **submission through an
insurance portal** is required · **the insurance requirements conflict with the contract**.

## 6. Three standing rules

**§10 — Do not send requests to multiple people.** *"To prevent duplication, confusion, and
delays, salespeople should not send the same contract or COI request to multiple internal
contacts."*

**§11 — Purchase orders may contain contract terms.** A PO must go to the Sales Manager when it
contains T&C, references online terms, requires a signature or acceptance, includes insurance
requirements, changes payment terms, includes retainage or penalties, conflicts with TCG's quote,
or refers to another agreement. *"**Do not assume that a purchase order is only an authorization
to begin work.**"*

> That directly qualifies the March 2026 lifecycle diagram, whose Type 1 customer PO is described
> as *"their internal authorization for the job… **no internal reporting required**"* —
> [quote-lifecycle-permits-and-purchase-orders.md](quote-lifecycle-permits-and-purchase-orders.md)
> §2. **The SOP says a PO may be a contract. The diagram says a PO is a reference number. Both are
> in the same drop and nothing reconciles them.**

**§12 — Existing customers are not exempt.** A previous contract or COI *"may not apply to every
new project"*. Re-route when: a new legal entity is involved · a new project-specific contract
arrives · the customer issues new T&C · **insurance requirements change** · a new certificate
holder is listed · new endorsements are requested · **the previous agreement may have expired** ·
a new PO with terms arrives.

**§13 — When in doubt**, email the Sales Manager and ask: *"Can you please confirm whether this
requires contract or insurance review before we proceed?"*

## 7. The COI Request Playbook — the mechanics

Ryan Adams' one-pager, 2026-05-11, Team **Sales**, Associated Value Engine **Leads**. It covers
the same five steps at the broker end rather than the internal end:

1. **Identify requirements** — from the contract: coverage amounts, policy types (**General
   Liability, Auto, Workers Compensation, Umbrella**) and endorsement language (**Additional
   Insured, Waiver of Subrogation, Primary & Non-Contributory**).
2. **Gather certificate holder details** — official name, mailing address, contact email;
   *"Verify spelling and legal entity names before submitting."*
3. **Contact the insurance agent/broker** — by email, with all project/job information, noting
   urgency.
4. **Provide necessary information** — certificate holder legal name and address, job/project
   name and location, policy information if requested, effective dates, and any special
   requirements (Additional Insured, Waiver of Subrogation, Primary & Non-Contributory, **specific
   cancellation notice requirements**).
5. **Request and confirm the Additional Insured endorsement** — *"confirm the client/entity is
   listed as an Additional Insured on the policy. **Review the completed COI carefully before
   sending** to ensure all requirements, endorsements, dates, and certificate holder information
   are accurate."*

**The broker is named** as AssuredPartners, with a separate address for contracts at Gallagher
(`ajg.com`). Three named broker contacts, their email addresses and an after-hours mobile number
are listed in the source — **deliberately not copied here; see
`3.1.1_MODULE-CRM-SALES-QUOTES/__ERP_Sales Process (from Ryan 2026-08-06)/COI Certificate of Insurance Request 1.docx`.**
The stated escalation is: *"If we need one rushed after hours (past 5 pm or before 7 am) we can
call John's cell."*

## 8. Responsibilities

| Salesperson | Sales Manager |
| --- | --- |
| **Recognizing when a document is a contract or COI request** · forwarding the complete request · including all attachments and customer instructions · providing accurate customer and project information · obtaining missing information from the customer · responding promptly · maintaining the customer relationship · **avoiding unauthorized promises or approvals** · keeping the Sales Manager informed of deadlines and job dates · forwarding customer questions or requested changes | **Receiving all contract and COI requests** · reviewing each submission for completeness · coordinating contract processing through the internal program · coordinating insurance and COI processing · identifying missing information · **coordinating redlines and required approvals** · communicating questions and status · returning completed documents · **confirming when the request is complete** |
