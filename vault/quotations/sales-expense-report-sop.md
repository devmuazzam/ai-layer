# Sales Expense Report SOP — routing through the Sales Manager

**What this is.** A short SOP owned by **Accounting**, applying to the **Sales Team**, whose whole
content is a routing change: sales expense reports now go to the **Sales Manager first**, not
straight to HR/Accounting.

> **This is administrative, not product.** It is filed under `quotations/` only because the
> governance set treats it as a Value Engine and because expenses are coded to a customer, job or
> quote. **It is more naturally payroll/accounting material** and a human may prefer it there.

---

## 1. The change

| Previous process | New process | Reason for change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales employees submitted expense reports **directly to HR** | Sales employees submit to the **Sales Manager first** for review and approval; the Sales Manager forwards approved reports to Accounting | *"This creates **one Sales-level checkpoint before Accounting** receives the report, helping catch missing receipts, unclear charges, policy issues, and coding questions before they slow down reimbursement or reconciliation."* |

**Scope** — *"all Sales Team expenses submitted for reimbursement or company card reconciliation,
including meals, customer visits, travel, parking, fuel, lodging, events, supplies, and other
approved business expenses."*

**Routing:** `Sales Employee → Sales Manager Review → Accounting Processing`.

## 2. The seven steps

1. **Complete the expense report** — *"Each line item must include the **date, vendor, amount,
   expense category, business purpose, and customer/job reference when applicable**."*
2. **Attach all receipts** — *"**Credit card screenshots alone should not replace itemized
   receipts** unless no itemized receipt is available and the reason is noted."*
3. **Submit to the Sales Manager** — *"The Sales Team should not send Sales expense reports
   directly to HR as the first step. **This is the main process change.**"*
4. **Sales Manager reviews** — for missing receipts, unclear business purpose, **duplicate
   charges**, customer/job support, policy concerns, and reasonableness.
5. **Mark Approved or Not Approved** — approved reports go on to Accounting; not-approved reports
   return to the employee with the required correction.
6. **Accounting processes only approved reports.**
7. **Employee corrects returned reports** and resubmits **to the Sales Manager**, not directly to
   Accounting.

## 3. The review checklist

☐ Expense report is complete and readable
☐ **All receipts are attached and match the amounts submitted**
☐ Each expense has a clear business purpose
☐ **Customer, job, quote, or project is identified when applicable**
☐ **Meal or entertainment expenses include who attended and why the meeting was business-related**
☐ Dates, vendors and amounts are accurate
☐ No duplicate or personal expenses are included
☐ Expense appears reasonable and aligned with company expectations
☐ Any unusual item has an explanation
☐ Report is marked Approved or Not Approved before being sent to Accounting

## 4. Submission deadline

> *"Please submit expense report to the Sales manager **the Wednesday after the calendar month
> ends**. Example: if the last day of the month is a Thursday please submit by the next
> Wednesday."*

## 5. What binds the ERP, if anything

Only one thing: **an expense line can carry a customer, job, quote or project reference**. That is
the same coding dimension `accounting/` would need for cost attribution, and it means a quote is
one of the things an expense can point at. Nothing here asks for the process to be built.

**Two states only** — `Approved` and `Not Approved` — with a return-to-employee loop and a
compliance note: *"Expense reports that are sent directly to Accounting without Sales Manager's
approval **may be returned and redirected** back through the approved process."*

The SOP also supplies two email templates (employee → Sales Manager, Sales Manager → Accounting)
and a rollout message, all with the subject-line patterns `Expense Report for Review - [Employee
Name] - [Date Range]` and `Approved Sales Expense Report - [Employee Name] - [Date Range]`.
